\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:Intro}
\noindent{\textbf{\huge T}}he modernization of agricultural supply chains represents a foundational socio-economic imperative for rapidly developing nations. In India, agriculture sustains the livelihoods of over 140 million smallholder farm households \cite{AgriCensus2022}. Peri-urban agricultural corridors---such as the northern periphery of Bengaluru spanning Yelahanka, Devanahalli, Doddaballapur, and Nelamangala, as well as the Mandya--Maddur corridor---play a pivotal role in daily urban food security by cultivating highly perishable nutritional staples including tomatoes, leafy greens, beans, brinjal, carrots, capsicum, and brassica varieties for dense urban agglomerations. Sustaining these critical peri-urban farming ecosystems requires equitable economic returns for smallholder cultivators, transparent market-clearing mechanisms, and timely access to scientific agronomic expertise.

However, the prevailing reality of agricultural commodity distribution is characterized by severe institutional and structural fragmentation. Farmgate transactions remain dominated by an entrenched, multi-tiered hierarchy of unorganized commission agents (\textit{arhatiyas}), village intermediaries, wholesalers, and regional sub-dealers \cite{NITIAayog2018}. These intermediaries exploit stark information asymmetries regarding real-time terminal mandi prices, depriving smallholder farmers of transparent price discovery. Furthermore, the absence of coordinated rural cold-chain aggregation and demand-responsive logistics results in protracted, multi-day transit delays (38--54 hours), causing 20\% to 40\% of harvested produce to degrade before reaching end consumers \cite{MoFPI2022}. Simultaneously, smallholder growers face steep linguistic and digital accessibility hurdles; existing agricultural advisories and e-trading portals rely predominantly on static, text-heavy interfaces that fail to accommodate vernacular regional dialects or rural literacy constraints.

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:traditional_apmc_flow}, produce passes through four to six intermediate intermediaries: Farmer $\rightarrow$ Mandi (APMC) $\rightarrow$ Commission Agent (\textit{Arhatiya}) $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler/Trader $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer. At each stage, mandi cess, handling commissions, and trader margins extract economic value ($\approx 10\%$ cumulative market fees alone), while the smallholder absorbs transportation costs and unstandardized manual weighment deductions. The direct consequence of this systemic market failure is an alarming socio-economic imbalance: smallholder farmers receive an exploitative 25\% to 35\% of the terminal consumer rupee, frequently enduring 45 to 60-day payment credit cycles or outright defaults, forcing distress sales below production costs during seasonal harvest peaks \cite{NITIAayog2018}. Conversely, urban consumers and commercial institutions pay inflated prices for degraded produce that has endured prolonged post-harvest handling. To decisively bridge these systemic fissures, this project proposes and implements \textbf{AgriLink}, an intelligent direct farm-to-consumer digital supply ecosystem harmonizing transparent algorithmic disintermediation, hybrid collaborative recommendation engines, multilingual voice-driven agronomic advisory, and coordinated rural micro-hub logistics.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:prob_stmt}
The core engineering problem addressed by AgriLink is the elimination of predatory intermediary overheads and linguistic barriers through an integrated cyber-physical marketplace architecture. Traditional Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) yards operate under oligopsonistic trading regimes where fragmented farmers possess virtually zero bargaining power \cite{Sedek2021}. Smallholders lack predictive visibility into forward market demand, resulting in recurrent boom-and-bust harvest gluts and catastrophic farmgate price crashes \cite{Rehman2024}. 

Moreover, as demonstrated by recent agricultural information systems literature \cite{Glaros2023, Koksal2019}, isolated software platforms fail because they separate transactional trading from operational knowledge transfer. Farmers receiving advisory on pest infestation through telephone helplines such as Kisan Call Centres (KCC) cannot seamlessly procure biological inputs or schedule harvest pickups. On the consumer side, retail and institutional buyers encounter high search frictions, lack of batch-level harvest traceability, and absence of personalized replenishment systems for perishable goods \cite{Sarwar2001, Ariannezhad2022}.

To formalize this challenge, AgriLink models the supply chain as a multi-tier optimization graph as depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:agrilink_context}. The platform directly connects verified farmers with rural aggregation hubs, route-optimized clean electric fleets, and end consumers under a mathematically regulated 68-10-14-8 revenue distribution model. As analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:prob_stmt}, replacing predatory middleman markups with verifiable smart contract logic guarantees that 68\% of every consumer rupee is directly disbursed to the grower's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) account. 

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:Obj}
To achieve comprehensive farm-to-consumer market disintermediation, automated intelligence, and transparent rural employment creation, the AgriLink platform is architected around four interdependent technical and socio-economic objectives, spanning fourteen functional modules (M1--M14) of the SFIC 2026 platform design:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Objective \arabic*:}, leftmargin=2.5cm]
	\item \textbf{Verified Multi-Sided Digital Marketplace with Enterprise Data and Four-Method Authentication Platform} (Modules M1--M6, M10--M14): Design and implement a production-grade, normalized Third Normal Form (3NF) and Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) PostgreSQL relational platform (Supabase) supporting five distinct multi-tenant roles --- Farmers, Retail Customers, Institutional Buyers, Rural Aggregators, and Delivery Partners --- with granular Row-Level Security (RLS). The marketplace incorporates a versatile four-method enterprise authentication suite comprising Google OAuth 2.0 with web callback routing, transactional Gmail SMTP 8-digit OTP verification enforcing strict \texttt{@gmail.com} pattern compliance, SMS OTP, and WhatsApp OTP (M1). It encompasses multilingual crop listing and harvest declaration (M2), AI-assisted catalogue discovery and cart management for retail and institutional buyers (M3, M4), aggregator hub quality grading, digital weighment, and batch consolidation (M5), full order lifecycle orchestration from placement to OTP-confirmed doorstep delivery (M6), automated payment settlement and review management (M10, M12), and a tamper-evident QR provenance traceability chain anchored on the Polygon Layer-2 ledger (M11), governed by continuous GitHub Actions CI/CD workflows and automated cloud deployment.

	\item \textbf{Seven-Model Hybrid AI/ML Recommendation Engine} (Module M9): Formulate and deploy an intelligent seven-model recommendation engine serving customers across two dashboard tiers. The Home Dashboard serves Buy Again recency-frequency scoring (Model~1), FP-Growth co-purchase association mining (Model~2), and User-Based Collaborative Filtering (Model~3). The Discover Dashboard serves Next-Basket Prediction (Model~4), Recency-Weighted exponential time-decay (Model~5), Price-Aware budget-fit hybrid (Model~6), and Local Popularity region-based trending (Model~7). The engine also provides farmers with forward 7-day demand forecasting and sowing guidance, and supports aggregators with AI-assisted produce photo grading, collectively overcoming acute cold-start fragility inherent in 48--72 hour perishable crop lifecycles.

	\item \textbf{Multilingual Voice-Driven Agricultural Advisory and Decoupled NLP Query Engine} (Module M9, M14): Construct an end-to-end conversational voice assistant and modular NLP engine (\texttt{nlp\_engine}) integrating the Flutter \texttt{speech\_to\_text} package across Indic locales, a TF-IDF bigram intent classifier covering six agricultural query classes, OpenAI Whisper Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), AI4Bharat IndicTrans2 neural machine translation for 22 official Indian languages, and dense FAISS vector retrieval over an agronomy knowledge corpus. The advisory pipeline delivers sub-3.5-second, dialect-aware crop advisory, pest identification guidance, real-time APMC price feeds, and farmer payout summaries with strict RLS data isolation in Kannada, Telugu, Hindi, Malayalam, Tamil, and English, with push and SMS notification support via M14.

	\item \textbf{Live APMC Mandi Ingestion, Algorithmic Fair-Share Pricing, and Rural Cloud Logistics Infrastructure} (Modules M7--M8, M13): Implement a transparent, lock-at-order algorithmic pricing engine ingesting real-time APMC mandi modal feeds from a live open-source cloud API, coupling a native scikit-learn 1.9+ Random Forest regressor ($R^2 = 0.9999$) against batch quality grades and verified organic certification. The engine enforces a constitutionally-protected 68-10-14-8 revenue distribution model that guarantees 68\% of every consumer rupee is disbursed directly to the cultivator's UPI account. Integrate Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) heuristics for electric three-wheeler route optimization across the 85~km Mandya--Bengaluru agricultural corridor, targeting post-harvest transit losses below 10\%. The microservice architecture is orchestrated across Render Cloud and GitHub Pages, governed by an Admin Console with real-time analytics (M13).
\end{enumerate}
