\chapter{Literature Survey}
\label{ch:Lit}
\noindent{\textbf{\huge T}}his chapter presents a systematic critical evaluation of state-of-the-art literature across two foundational computational domains: (i) digital agricultural marketplaces, recommender architectures, and cloud data platforms; and (ii) speech-driven multilingual natural language processing and retrieval-augmented decision support for rural farming communities. Through rigorous benchmarking against existing research paradigms and deployed commercial platforms, we identify structural operational gaps in contemporary solutions and articulate the formal motivation underpinning AgriLink.

\section{Recommendation in Grocery and Agricultural Commerce}
\label{sec:LitRev_rec}
Recommendation systems in agricultural and grocery commerce differ fundamentally from conventional media or book recommenders. Grocery shopping exhibits high repeat consumption, multi-item baskets, tight price sensitivity, and strict perishability constraints.

\subsection{Collaborative Filtering and Matrix Factorization}
Collaborative filtering (CF) uses interaction patterns across users and items to predict preferences. In user-based CF \cite{Resnick1994, Sarwar2001}, each customer is represented by an interaction vector $\mathbf{x}_u$. The cosine similarity between two users $u$ and $v$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\text{cosine}(u, v) = \frac{\mathbf{x}_u^\top \mathbf{x}_v}{\|\mathbf{x}_u\|_2 \|\mathbf{x}_v\|_2}
\label{eqn:cosine_lit}
\end{equation}
Products purchased by nearest neighbors $\mathcal{N}(u)$ that have not yet been purchased by user $u$ can then be scored and recommended. Matrix factorization techniques, notably Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) formalized by Koren et al. \cite{Koren2009, Koren2010}, map both customers and produce listings into a joint latent factor space of dimensionality $k$. While highly interpretable and effective for established items, collaborative filtering suffers severe degradation when applied to perishable produce due to the acute cold-start problem: a freshly harvested batch of tomatoes listed 3 hours ago has zero prior ratings or transaction history.

\subsection{Next-Basket Recommendation (NBR) and Repeat Consumption}
Unlike static Top-$N$ recommendation, Next-Basket Recommendation (NBR) explicitly predicts the next ordered basket based on a chronologically ordered sequence of prior transactions \cite{Yu2016}. In grocery shopping, a substantial proportion of orders consist of repeat purchases. Ariannezhad et al. \cite{Ariannezhad2022} formulated \textit{ReCANet}, a repeat consumption-aware neural network demonstrating that explicitly modeling recently and frequently purchased items significantly outperforms general sequential models. Faggioli et al. \cite{Faggioli2020} investigated recency-aware collaborative filtering for online grocery shopping, demonstrating that applying time-decay weighting to user interactions captures seasonal and perishable replenishment cycles far more accurately than unweighted models.

\subsection{Association Rule Mining and Co-Purchase Bundling}
Association-rule mining discovers products frequently co-purchased within the same transaction basket. Agrawal and Srikant \cite{Agrawal1994} established the Apriori principle, which was subsequently optimized by Han et al. \cite{Han2000} via the Frequent Pattern Growth (FP-Growth) algorithm. FP-Growth compresses transactional datasets into an FP-tree and mines frequent itemsets without candidate generation. Association rules $A \Rightarrow B$ are evaluated using support, confidence, and lift:
\begin{equation}
\text{support}(A \Rightarrow B) = P(A \cup B), \quad \text{confidence}(A \Rightarrow B) = \frac{P(A \cup B)}{P(A)}, \quad \text{lift}(A \Rightarrow B) = \frac{P(A \cup B)}{P(A)P(B)}
\end{equation}
Co-purchase rules are invaluable for real-time cart upsell and bundle recommendations (e.g., suggesting coriander and green chillies when onions and tomatoes are in the cart).

\subsection{Sequential, Transformer, and Graph-Based Recommenders}
Advanced recommendation paradigms have explored sequential neural representations and graph neural networks. Sun et al. \cite{Sun2019} proposed \textit{BERT4Rec}, adapting bidirectional Transformer encoders to model user behavior sequences with Cloze masking. In graph collaborative filtering, He et al. \cite{He2020} introduced \textit{LightGCN}, showing that removing feature transformations and non-linear activations from graph convolutional networks yields superior collaborative embeddings through neighborhood propagation. Kula \cite{Kula2015} developed \textit{LightFM}, a hybrid matrix factorization model representing users and items as linear combinations of their metadata features, effectively bridging collaborative filtering and content-based recommendation for cold-start listings.

\section{Multilingual NLP and Voice Advisory Systems}
\label{sec:LitRev_nlp}
Providing localized, voice-driven agronomic guidance to rural farming communities is critical to overcoming literacy and linguistic exclusion. Rehman et al. \cite{Rehman2024} developed \textit{KisanQRS}, an automated query-response system trained on 34 million Kisan Call Centre (KCC) call logs. Evaluating over 300,000 samples, their LSTM query mapper achieved an F1-score of 96.58\% and NDCG of 96.20\%. However, KisanQRS was designed purely as a detached advisory portal with no integration into a commercial trading platform.

Rahman et al. \cite{Rahman2025} built a speech-driven conversational assistant using fine-tuned OpenAI Whisper-base ASR, Sentence-Transformers, a FAISS vector database, and 4-bit quantized Llama-2-7B. Tested on extension agronomy documents, the system achieved an 18.5\% Word Error Rate (WER) and 3.5-second latency. Abhishek et al. \cite{Abhishek2025} developed \textit{AgriTalk}, integrating AI4Bharat IndicTrans2 across 22 official Indian languages with dense multilingual MiniLM embeddings, validating high translation fidelity in Kannada, Telugu, Hindi, and Malayalam. Reddy et al. \cite{Reddy2025} developed \textit{Agri Assist}, combining stacked ensemble regressors for soil-crop yield forecasting with FastText embeddings (99.32\% accuracy). Biswas and Goyal \cite{Biswas2023} combined Sentence-Transformers with Pegasus summarization to ingest Agmarknet mandi feeds, demonstrating 96.0\% question-mapping precision.

\section{Identified Research Gaps and Motivation}
\label{sec:Motivation}
A synthesis of existing academic literature and industrial systems reveals four critical, unaddressed research gaps:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Gap \arabic*:}, leftmargin=2cm]
	\item \textbf{The Transaction-Advisory Silo}: Existing systems treat agricultural guidance and market commerce as disjoint domains. Farmers consult disconnected helplines \cite{Rehman2024} or standalone chatbots \cite{Rahman2025, Abhishek2025} for agronomic advice, but are forced to surrender to exploitative APMC commission agents to sell their actual harvest. AgriLink bridges this gap by directly embedding multilingual voice advisory into an active commercial clearinghouse.
	
	\item \textbf{Linguistic Exclusion and Acoustic Dialect Fragility}: While Indian agriculture spans over twenty major linguistic families, existing commercial apps remain heavily English-centric. Dialect-aware models \cite{Abhishek2025} have largely remained laboratory prototypes detached from live market prices. AgriLink integrates fine-tuned Whisper ASR with AI4Bharat IndicTrans2 across Kannada, Telugu, Hindi, Malayalam, and English, directly wired to real-time Agmarknet mandi feeds.
	
	\item \textbf{Cold-Start Fragility in Perishable Produce Recommendation}: Conventional recommendation models \cite{Sarwar2001, Koren2009} assume static, long-lived item catalogs. In agricultural supply, fresh produce exhibits short 48-to-72 hour lifecycles. AgriLink overcomes this through a seven-model hybrid recommendation engine combining collaborative filtering with content-based fallback, FP-Growth co-purchase rules, Next-Basket Prediction, recency decay, price fitting, and local popularity.
	
	\item \textbf{Economic Exploitation and Opacity in Middleman Chains}: Smallholders capture less than 35\% of the terminal consumer rupee due to multi-layered broker markups. AgriLink bridges this structural injustice through an algorithmic 68-10-14-8 fair-share economic distribution model, combining digital quality assay receipts with tamper-proof Layer-2 smart ledger verification to guarantee that 68\% of every transaction is instantly and irreversibly disbursed to the cultivator.
\end{enumerate}
