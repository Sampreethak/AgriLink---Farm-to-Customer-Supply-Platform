\chapter{Results and Discussions}
\label{ch:Results}
\noindent {\textbf{\huge T}}his chapter presents the empirical evaluation, quantitative performance benchmarks, and comparative discussions of the AgriLink platform. We evaluate five core dimensions of the system: (i) recommendation accuracy and ranking fidelity of the seven-model engine under cold-start and warm-start conditions; (ii) extended model performance for the Discover dashboard; (iii) multilingual speech recognition, NLP intent classification, and advisory response latency; (iv) backend throughput and database response performance under heavy concurrent workloads; and (v) microeconomic welfare gains comparing AgriLink against traditional APMC mandi supply chains.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:Res}
The quantitative validation of AgriLink was conducted using a pilot dataset comprising 4,250+ verified transaction, harvest, and interaction records collected across the North Bengaluru peri-urban agricultural corridor (spanning Devanahalli, Doddaballapur, Yelahanka, and Hebbal).

(1) Modular NLP Unit Tests; (2) Farmer Conversational Retrieval \& Vernacular Tests; (3) Farmer Row-Level Security (RLS) Isolation; (4) Dynamic Pricing Regressor \& Mandi APMC Feed; (5) Multi-Model Recommendation Engine Contracts; and (6) Full System Regression Suite. The automated deployment pipeline successfully pushed the FastAPI ML microservice to Render Cloud and the Flutter Web application to GitHub Pages.

\section{Discussions}
\label{sec:Disc}

The empirical results substantiate the architectural hypotheses established in Chapter~\ref{ch:Intro}. In the recommendation domain, standalone collaborative filtering exhibits severe degradation when applied to perishable goods because freshly harvested batches carry no prior user ratings. Pure SVD achieved an NDCG@10 of only 0.784, frequently recommending out-of-stock historical items. Conversely, AgriLink's dual-stage architecture leverages content-based TF-IDF feature projections during the first 12 hours of batch availability, smoothly transitioning to latent collaborative factors as interactions accumulate. This hybrid mechanism achieved an NDCG@10 of 0.962, representing a statistically significant improvement over all baselines and aligning closely with findings by Rehman et al.~\cite{Rehman2024}.

The four extended Discover models (Models 4--7) collectively achieved an average NDCG@10 of 0.880 with 100\% cold-start coverage, demonstrating that temporal, budget, and geographic signals provide orthogonal and complementary discovery value over the core collaborative models. In particular, Local Popularity (Model 7) achieved the highest NDCG@10 of 0.903, confirming that zone-based trending is highly predictive for fresh produce in geographically clustered agricultural corridors.

The NLP intent classifier achieved a macro-average accuracy of 95.6\% using TF-IDF cosine similarity, with the \texttt{PEST\_DISEASE\_ADVISORY} intent achieving 98.6\% due to the distinctive and unambiguous vocabulary of agronomic pest terminology. The speech recognition pipeline achieved a macro-average WER of 17.46\% and end-to-end latency of 3.04 seconds, comparing favorably with Rahman et al.~\cite{Rahman2025} (WER 18.5\%, latency 3.5 s), with particular improvements in Kannada and Hindi dialects through lexicon fine-tuning.

From an infrastructure perspective, the asynchronous FastAPI and Supabase PostgreSQL architecture demonstrated exceptional concurrency resilience, achieving a p95 latency of 224 ms under 500 simultaneous connections. Most crucially, the microeconomic evaluation confirms algorithmic disintermediation directly resolves the market failures identified in Section~\ref{sec:prob_stmt}: farmers achieve income uplift exceeding 40\% while consumers enjoy a 20\% price saving on fresh, fully traceable produce. These findings demonstrate that technological intervention can simultaneously achieve pro-farmer equity, consumer affordability, and environmental sustainability.
